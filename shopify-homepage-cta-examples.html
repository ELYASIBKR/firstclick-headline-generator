Free Shopify homepage guide

A better homepage button tells shoppers what happens next.

“Shop now” can work, but it gives no clue about the next screen. Write the button for the destination you actually have. These five examples are fictional patterns, not real store copy or performance claims.

1 · One clear collection

Help shoppers browse a named category.

Shop cleaning refills

Open: A cleaning-refills collection with relevant products visible immediately.

Not: Your all-products page with unrelated goods above the refills. If that is the only page you have, use a broader button or create the matching collection first.

2 · One featured product

Let shoppers inspect the item before choosing.

See the rain jacket

Open: That jacket's product page, where sizes, materials, price, and delivery information can be checked.

Not: A direct-to-checkout link if the shopper has not chosen a size or seen the key details.

3 · A useful way to narrow choices

Promise a guided step only when it exists.

Browse tea by flavour

Open: A tea collection with flavour filters or clear flavour groupings.

Not: An unfiltered catalog. “Find your perfect tea” also overpromises if there is no quiz, guide, or meaningful way to narrow the choice.

4 · An unfamiliar offer

Give shoppers the explanation they need.

How refills work

Open: A concise explanation of what arrives, what is reused, and how a refill is ordered.

Not: A general About page that makes shoppers search for the answer. Use this as a secondary button if the primary goal is shopping.

5 · A real limited offer

Make the terms visible before asking for the click.

View the bundle

Open: The actual bundle page with included items and current price. If you mention a saving, its basis and terms should be clear and accurate.

Not: “Claim my discount” when the destination has no applicable discount. Do not invent urgency, scarcity, or savings to make the button louder.

The 30-second CTA check

  1. Read the headline and button together. Do they describe the same product and action?
  2. Tap the button on a phone. Does the destination match the button's exact promise?
  3. Can a first-time shopper see the next decision without hunting through menus?
  4. Check price, availability, delivery, and offer language against the live page before publishing.
